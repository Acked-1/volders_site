Modules

Seven utilities, free and open-source. Six of them are polyphonic. All available in the VCV Library under the Volders brand.

LFO
Free
LFO-01
4 HP
Four waveforms, slow/fast range switch, reset trigger. Exponential frequency knob so equal twists give equal ratio changes.
VCV Library →
VCA
Free
VCA-01
4 HP · Polyphonic
Linear or exponential response. CV is normalled open, so it passes signal the moment you patch it rather than sitting silent.
VCV Library →
Envelope
Free
ENV-01
6 HP · Polyphonic
ADSR from 1 ms to 10 seconds. Every polyphony channel gets its own envelope, so a sixteen-note chord runs sixteen independent shapes.
VCV Library →
Mixer
Free
MIX-01
8 HP · Polyphonic
Four channels with individual levels and a clip indicator. The mix is as polyphonic as its widest input.
VCV Library →
Clock
Free
CLK-01
4 HP
Clock with /1, /2, /4 and /8 division. Reset clears the divider count as well as the phase, so a divided output lands back on the beat.
VCV Library →
Noise / S&H
Free
NSE-01
6 HP
White or pink noise plus a sample & hold. The S&H input is normalled to the internal noise, so a trigger alone gives you random voltages.
VCV Library →
Utility
Free
ATT-01
4 HP · Polyphonic
Attenuvert through a centre zero, then add up to ±5V of offset. With nothing patched it becomes a manual DC source.
VCV Library →

About

I'm just someone who loves tinkering. New things, new challenges — that's what keeps it interesting. Music and electronics crossed paths at some point and I never looked back.

The Korg Volca series got me hooked on hardware. Then came the Elektron Digitakt and Syntakt, and it clicked — buttons, knobs, patch cables. No menus, no presets. Just hands-on.

Building VCV Rack modules is one of those things I love doing. It started with one LFO and turned into a small range of utilities, all free. The bigger modules are where the tinkering is taking me next.
